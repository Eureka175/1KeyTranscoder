# P4.9B — Controlled Residual Calibration & Uncertainty Study

**Scope**: audio only. No production algorithm, threshold or default is changed.
This phase supplies independent evidence about the size of the measurement and
application error, keeps the three corpora apart, and states precisely what would
still be needed before a residual acceptance criterion (D3) could exist.

```text
corpus A  20260903 / 20260904 short clips        (P4.9A, measured there)
corpus B  C1305 / C1306 long clips               (P4.7/P4.8 + this phase)
corpus C  deterministic known-truth synthetic    (this phase, R1/R3)
```

**The three are never pooled into one residual.** A number computed over a mixture
of them would describe the mixture and nothing else.

## 1. R1 — known-truth end-to-end residual

Every case is carried through the whole path, and the stages are reported
separately so that "the correction was applied" never stands in for "the
correction was right":

```text
source PCM (known truth D(k))
  -> production measurement    core.sync_estimate.estimate_pair
  -> production evidence gate  thresholds READ from core.channel_sync.effective_opts
  -> production plan           core.audio_sync_tv_exec.build_time_varying_plan
  -> actual PCM correction     core.audio_sync_tv_exec.run_time_varying(verify=True)
  -> independent verifier      plain full-rate FFT correlation, no GCC-PHAT
  -> known-truth residual      on the constant-delay population only
```

### 1.1 The independent verifier had a lag-axis defect, and it was fixed here

R1 requires that the verifier not share the estimator's core computation. It does
not — but it was **wrong**, and the way it was wrong is worth recording because it
would have made every known-truth residual meaningless.

The first verifier folded the correlation surface with

```python
lags = np.concatenate((np.arange(0, n), np.arange(-n, 0)))
order = np.argsort(lags)         # <- negative lags end up FIRST
```

so bin `i` did not correspond to `lags[i]` for negative lags. The correct reading
is `c[m]` for `m >= 0` and `c[nfft + m]` for `m < 0`, where `nfft` is the **padded**
length. Measured consequences, on material whose shift was known exactly:

| known shift applied to the target | true delay | first verifier | corrected verifier |
|---|---|---|---|
| target advanced 320 | −320 | **+46.979** | −320.000 |
| target advanced 137 | −137 | **+13.979** | −137.000 |
| target delayed 137 | +137 | +137.000 | +137.000 |
| no shift | 0 | +0.350 | 0.000 |

The defect is asymmetric: negative delays survived, positive delays did not. That
asymmetry is why it first looked like a small constant bias (`+0.35 samples`, the
same value in every case at zero delay) rather than a broken axis. The reproducer
is `work/p4.9b/diag_lag_exact.py`, the fix is verified against an explicit
per-lag brute-force correlation in `work/p4.9b/diag_lag_fix.py`, and
`diag_verifier_bias.py` is the measurement that exposed it.

**The same defect existed in P4.9A's verifier**, which was written the same way.
It was checked against this phase's fix (`diag_p49a_verifier.py`):

* on **negative** delays the two agree exactly (`CH3→CH1` 1185.811 both,
  `CH3→CH2` 857.488 both on the clip tested) — and every P4.9A
  `CH3→CH1` / `CH3→CH2` number is on that side, so **those published results
  stand**;
* on near-zero positive delays they differ by 1.416 samples, which is exactly the
  region in which P4.9A's `CH3→CH4` residuals (0.461 vs −0.954) were reported.
  Those residual figures are superseded by this phase's, and the correction is
  recorded here rather than quietly applied.

### 1.3 What R1 established

```text
cases                       15
  ELIGIBLE / APPLIED        14
  REFUSED (correct)         1
mechanics                   {'EXACT': 14, 'NOT_RUN': 1}
estimate error, constant    [0.0]  samples
known-truth residual, const max |residual| = 0.00000178 samples
controls fired              3 of 3
```

**The estimator's integer error is exactly zero on every constant-delay case, and
the known-truth residual after correction is exactly zero.** Both are the correct
result for an integer-delay estimator on an integer-delay fixture, and both are
worth stating precisely because they are the *only* thing R1's constant cases can
show: mechanical and numeric exactness. They say nothing about real material.

**The residual is not zero because the material is easy.** It is zero because the
correction is an integer shift and the truth is an integer shift, so no
quantisation remains. That is a statement about the mechanism, and the mechanism is
the part this R1 is able to certify.

The piecewise cases are where the interesting error lives, and it is **not** an
estimator error: `mechanics = EXACT` on all of them, i.e. the applier read exactly
the sample the contract prescribed. The error appears in
`coordinate error`, comparing the applied per-sample shift against the truth:

* `R1-piecewise-2region` and `R1-piecewise-ramp` — **0 samples of error**. When the
  delay changes exactly on the plan's own 10 s grid, the per-window estimate
  tracks it and the correction is exact.
* `R1-piecewise-3region` — max **510** samples of error on 960000 samples.
* `R1-transition-in-window` — max **500** samples, on a step deliberately placed in
  the MIDDLE of a plan window: that window's median delay is a mixture of the two
  regimes, so half the window is corrected by the wrong value.
* `R1-transition-back` — max **700** samples.

So the measured boundary of the fixed-shift approach is not measurement accuracy
at all. It is the **within-window transition**: a single shift per 30 s window
cannot represent a delay that changes inside that window, and the error is bounded
by the size of the step.

The refusal case behaved exactly as the contract requires: `R1-gap-reentry`
(a silent stretch removes every measurable frame) reached
`apply = NOT_APPLIED` with `reason = audio_sync_tv_no_trusted_window`, and left no
output. Nothing was forced, and the refusal is counted as the correct outcome.

### What binds, per corpus

* **Short clips (corpus A).** The binding term is `min_usable_fraction`:
  loosening it to 0.4 and below takes `gate OK` from 4/6 to **6/6**, while the
  accepted-frame count and the residual do not change at all (4933 frames,
  residual median 0.00, p95 2.00). Raising it to 0.8 takes `gate OK` down to 1/6.
  `min_confidence` matters only above 0.5 (at 0.7 it costs 428 accepted frames and
  two sources), `frame_min_rms_dbfs` only above −40, and `min_usable_frames` does
  not bind anywhere in 1…100.
* **Long clips (corpus B).** The binding term is the **finite-frame count itself**,
  which no gate can change: coverage is 0.0040 at the production gate and
  **identical** at every coverage threshold from 0.0 to 0.8, and at every
  `min_usable_frames` from 1 to 100. Only `COV-0.00` admits the sources (6/6), and
  it admits them by removing the coverage requirement entirely — which is not an
  improvement in measurement, it is the absence of a criterion. The accepted
  population is ~50–700 finite frames out of 41 645–48 072, and remains so.
* **Coverage and residual move independently.** On corpus A the coverage goes from
  0.6397 to 0.5757 as `min_confidence` rises to 0.7 while the residual median stays
  0.00 and p95 stays 2.00 — tighter acceptance did not make the accepted frames
  agree better. On corpus B, `CONF-0.70` *raises* the residual median from 15.50 to
  148.00 samples. **A higher coverage number is not a higher accuracy**, and this
  is the measurement that shows it rather than the assertion.

**Nothing here is written back to production.** The production gate remains
`frames >= 5, confidence >= 0.3, coverage >= 0.6`, unchanged, and the statement
"corpus B cannot reach it" is a property of the material, not of the threshold —
which is exactly why lowering the threshold was not proposed.

## 3. R3 — uncertainty

### 3.1 First, the question that has to be settled by the code

`uncertainty_samples` **does not exist in the production path.** Measured here,
not remembered:

```text

reports_uncertainty              : False
```

The formula `uncertainty_samples = max(0.3, 0.5 * peak_width / sqrt(peak_over_noise))`
appears only in `docs/audio-sync-p3/validity.md` and
`docs/audio-sync-p3/PRODUCTION-REQUIREMENTS.md` — research records. There is no
production field to calibrate, so R3's question as posed ("is `uncertainty_samples`
statistically calibrated or only a physical bound?") has a prior answer:

```text
R3 = INSUFFICIENT_DATA for the calibration of a production uncertainty figure,
     because no production uncertainty figure is emitted.
```

P3 already recorded this as `UNCERTAINTY CALIBRATION = PARTIAL`; this phase confirms
it against the shipped code and adds the part that was missing: an actual
error-versus-truth distribution, which is what any future uncertainty figure would
have to be calibrated against.

### 3.3 What this supports, and what it does not

**Supported.** At the production geometry, on 20 s of band-limited material with a
constant integer delay, the estimator's error is **at most 1 sample** across
10233 frames and 52 non-empty cases, with a median absolute error of 0 in
most of them. The integer estimator is exact or within one sample of exact on this
class of material. That is a real, reproducible bound and it is the raw material any
uncertainty figure would need.

**Also supported — and it is the reason a physical bound is not an uncertainty.**
The eight cases with **zero** measurable frames have the *narrowest* bands and the
*cleanest* signal (no added noise). Their peak half-width is 12–26 samples against
2–14 for the wider bands. A narrow band makes the correlation peak broad and low,
so the estimator's own `success` condition (`peak >= 2 x noise floor`) fails on
every frame — the frames are not wrong, they are **refused**. An uncertainty figure
derived from peak width would be *largest* exactly where the estimator returns
nothing, and would be *smallest* where it returns an exact answer. Reported as an
error bar on a returned value, a peak-width bound would therefore describe the
wrong population.

**Not supported.** Nothing here says the estimator is calibrated on real material.
Every case is synthetic, noiseless or noise-added at a stated SNR, with a constant
integer delay, at one geometry, on one machine. The corpus-A and corpus-B results
in §2 and §4 are the counterweight: on real material the accepted population is
tiny and the frame-to-frame spread is hundreds of samples, which no synthetic
distribution here reproduces.

**P(|error| <= k x half-width)** was computed for k = 1, 2, 3 and is present in
`work/p4.9b/r3-uncertainty/per-case.jsonl`. It is **not** quoted as a calibration
result, because the quantity being tested is not a production uncertainty figure
and quoting a coverage ratio against a formula that ships nowhere would manufacture
the appearance of a calibration that has not happened.

## 4. R4 — independent verifier disagreement

Four separate questions, four separate answers. Nothing is averaged.

### 4.1 On known truth, the FIXED verifier is more accurate than the estimator

On the 60 R3 cases (identical material, identical truth):

```text
independent |error| : median 0.0000  p90 0.0001  max 0.0002 samples
production  |error| : median 0.0000  max 1.0000 samples
```

That is expected rather than surprising, and the reason is stated so it is
not read as a ranking: the verifier works at full rate with parabolic
sub-sample refinement on a single whole-clip correlation, while the
production estimator is an INTEGER estimator (its output is whole samples by
construction). Comparing them on sub-sample accuracy compares two different
kinds of object. The verifier is there to CHECK the estimator, not to beat it.

### 4.2 Can a quality metric flag an unreliable verifier answer?

```text
independent peak_to_sidelobe : 2.260 .. 136.825 (median 15.095)
peak half-width (samples)    : 2 .. 26 (median 8)
peak_over_noise              : 173 .. 1141 (median 427)
cases with |error| > 1 sample: 0 of 60
```

**On this material the question cannot be answered, and that is the honest
result.** There are no unreliable answers to test a screener against: every
case has |error| <= 1 sample, and even the least favourable case has a
peak-to-sidelobe ratio of 2.26, above the verifier's own 1.5 qualification threshold. A
metric that flags nothing on a population with nothing to flag has not been
shown to work; it has been shown to be untested.

### 4.3 The screener question where the answers WERE ambiguous: real material

The place where the independent estimate was genuinely unreliable in this
research line is real material — corpus B's `CH3→CH1` / `CH3→CH2`, where the
block-to-block spread reached hundreds of samples. P4.9A recorded, for every
one of its 411 cases, both the verifier's peak-to-sidelobe ratio and the
spread of its own 4 s block lags. Joining those two numbers asks the question
directly: does a low peak-to-sidelobe ratio identify the cases whose verifier
answer moves?

152 P4.9A cases carry both a qualified verifier answer and a
block-lag spread. `ambiguous` is defined as a block-lag spread > 10 samples,
which is the condition under which the verifier's single number does not
describe the clip.

```text
cases with spread <= 10 samples : 111  peak_to_sidelobe median   5.023
cases with spread >  10 samples :  41  peak_to_sidelobe median   2.083
```

| peak_to_sidelobe < | flagged | of which ambiguous | false alarms | missed ambiguous |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.5 | 13 | 11 | 2 | 30 |
| 2.0 | 33 | 19 | 14 | 22 |
| 2.5 | 49 | 28 | 21 | 13 |
| 3.0 | 66 | 35 | 31 | 6 |
| 4.0 | 81 | 38 | 43 | 3 |
| 5.0 | 96 | 41 | 55 | 0 |
| 10.0 | 141 | 41 | 100 | 0 |

The metric does separate the two populations — the median peak-to-sidelobe
ratio is 5.02 on the well-described cases against 2.08 on the ambiguous ones — but the sweep shows it is not a
usable gate at any single threshold: raising the threshold to catch the
ambiguous cases costs an increasing number of false alarms, and lowering it
misses most of them. Reported as a **weak screener, not a decision rule**.
No threshold here is proposed, applied, or written into production.

### 4.4 The P3.11 case

P3.11's `C1306 CH3→CH2` disagreement (independent residual ≈ +70 samples,
`fine_fit_r2 ≈ -0.21`) is **not reproduced or re-adjudicated here**, and it
cannot be: that pair does not reach the evidence gate on this material (R2:
coverage 0.0040, gate OK on 0 of 6 sources), so there is no eligible
measurement to compare a verifier against.

```text
P3.11 C1306 CH3->CH2 disagreement : UNRESOLVED (unchanged)
reason                            : the pair cannot reach the evidence gate
                                    on this material, so a verifier
                                    disagreement has no eligible population
                                    to be adjudicated against
```

A low `fine_fit_r2` is reported everywhere in this phase as a diagnostic
warning and never as proof that the verifier is wrong. Where the two could be
compared against a KNOWN truth (R1, R3, and §4.1 above), the fixed verifier
agreed with the truth to within 0.0002 samples and the estimator was exact at
integer precision — so on the material that can be adjudicated, there is no
disagreement left to explain.

### 4.5 The verifier defect, and what it does to published numbers

The lag-axis defect in §1.1 is a separate finding, not an explanation of
P3.11. Its signature is one-sidedness: negative delays survived, positive
delays did not.

* known synthetic shifts wrong under the P4.9A implementation: **3 of 7**
* on the real clip tested, P4.9A's verifier and the fixed one agree to within
  2 samples on every pair:

| pair | P4.9A verifier | corrected verifier | delta | agree |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `CH3->CH1` | 1185.811 | 1185.811 | +0.000 | yes |
| `CH3->CH2` | 857.488 | 857.488 | -0.000 | yes |
| `CH3->CH4` | 0.461 | -0.954 | +1.416 | yes |

Every `CH3→CH1` / `CH3→CH2` figure P4.9A published sits on the negative-delay
side, where the two implementations agree exactly, so **those results stand**.
The `CH3→CH4` residuals sat on the near-zero positive side, where they differ
by 1.416 samples; they are superseded by R1's exactly-zero residuals and the
correction is recorded rather than quietly applied.

This phase was not asked to produce a D3 number, and it does not. R1–R4 were all
executed; every result is traceable to a file under `work/p4.9b/`; the regression
roster is green with the historical failures carried. What is missing is not
effort, it is a kind of evidence:

**1. A residual figure that means the same thing on all three corpora.** The three
produce residuals that are not comparable, so no single acceptance number can be
derived from them:

| corpus | what the residual is | measured value |
|---|---|---|
| C (synthetic known truth) | true correction error | **0 samples** on constant delays; 0–700 samples on within-window transitions, bounded by the step |
| A (short real clips) | error of a correction that was never applied — the tracks are already aligned | `|delay| < 0.05 ms` by construction |
| B (long real clips) | not measurable: the pair carrying the delay cannot reach the gate | coverage 0.0040, gate OK 0/6 |

A threshold would have to be justified across all three, and the middle row is a
residual of *zero because nothing needed correcting*, which is not a measurement
of accuracy. Corpus B provides no eligible population at all.

**2. A production uncertainty figure to calibrate.** `uncertainty_samples` is not
emitted by any production module (§3.1, re-measured against the code). An
acceptance criterion of the form "accept when residual <= k x uncertainty" cannot
exist while the second term does not.

**3. An accuracy measurement on REAL material with a known truth.** Every exact
result in this phase is on synthetic material. On real material this line of work
has no truth to compare against — that is precisely why P4.9A could only say "the
CH3/CH4 pair is already aligned" and could not say what the residual *is* on the
pairs that carry the delay. The missing artefact is a real recording whose delay
was measured independently, e.g. by a clap/sync-event or a second instrument.

**4. A population large enough to set a quantile.** Setting an acceptance
threshold means choosing where in a distribution to cut. The only real-material
populations available are 88 synthetic-eligible short clips that needed no
correction, and 0 eligible long clips.

**What would establish D3.** Concretely, and in priority order:

1. real material where the off-camera feed is present and loud on the measured
   channel (so `silent_track` does not fire) and long enough for the gate;
2. an independent truth for that material's delay — a sync event, a clap, or a
   known injected offset in a real recording;
3. then, a residual distribution measured over that material, and a product
   decision about which quantile of it is acceptable.

**What this phase does establish, and it is not nothing.** The correction
mechanics are exact: on every applied case the output equals the contract's own
per-sample expectation (14/14 `EXACT`), and on constant known-truth delays the
residual is exactly zero. The estimator's integer error is zero on the synthetic
population and bounded by 1 sample over 10 233 frames across five bandwidths and
three SNRs. And the boundary of the fixed-shift approach is now measured rather
than assumed: it is not accuracy, it is the **within-window transition**, where a
single shift per 30 s window cannot represent a delay that changes inside it and
the error is bounded by the size of the step (500–700 samples in the fixtures
tested).

One caveat is recorded so it cannot be read as a licence: the synthetic
error bound of 1 sample is a property of clean, band-limited, CONSTANT-delay
material. It is not a bound on real wireless material, and corpus B is the
counter-example that shows the difference.
